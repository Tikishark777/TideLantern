The instrument

Two screens. Everything at a glance.

Leave the clock running while the other display shows tide predictions, marine forecasts or alternates between them.

01 · TIDE DISPLAY

See the next turn of the tide

Upcoming high and low events, predicted heights, a smooth schematic curve and a moving NOW marker. U.S. tidal data from NOAA CO-OPS.

02 · MARINE CONDITIONS

Wind, waves, water

Modeled wind speed and gusts, wave heights and sea-surface temperature where forecast data is available. Powered by Open-Meteo.

03 · WORLD CLOCK

The right time, where you are

Location-aware time zone, 12/24-hour format, optional seconds and U.S., international or ISO dates on the left display.

Worldwide search ≠ worldwide tide coverage. Search for harbors globally; NOAA high/low predictions currently support U.S. NOAA stations only. When no appropriate station exists, TideLantern clearly shows that tidal predictions are unavailable; marine forecasts can still work. Additional international tide providers are planned.

Install TideLantern

Connect your 30-pin ESP32 with a USB data cable to a computer running Chrome or Edge. Use the browser installer to flash the latest TideLantern preview firmware.

Use desktop Chrome or Edge with Web Serial support.

New install can erase your ESP32 Wi-Fi settings. Do not flash your working SkyOrb unless you intend to replace it. First builds should be hardware-tested before daily use.

Phone setup

  1. Assemble the two GC9A01 displays using the wiring table below.
  2. Flash your ESP32 with the installer above.
  3. Connect your phone to TideLantern-Setup (password Tides2026!).
  4. Open http://192.168.4.1/setup. Tap Scan nearby Wi-Fi, search the list if needed, select your 2.4 GHz router and enter its password. Hidden networks can be typed manually.
  5. Press Save Wi-Fi & restart. If your phone reports no Internet while on the setup hotspot, stay connected until settings are saved.
  6. Reconnect your phone to your regular home Wi-Fi. Read the SETTINGS PAGE IP address shown at the bottom of the left clock screen.
  7. Open http:// plus that IP in your phone's browser (for example, http://192.168.1.123/; your address will be different). You can also try http://tidelantern.local/, although not all networks support this shortcut.
  8. Search your waterway, choose a nearby NOAA station if available, and select your display preferences.

Hardware

Same wiring. New marine experience.

One ESP-WROOM-32 (30-pin board), two 1.28-inch 240×240 GC9A01 SPI displays, 3.3V power. These are the exact original SkyOrb-compatible pins—no rewiring required.

Important: Verify your own display module specifications. These pin assignments and 3.3V power refer to the particular 7-pin GC9A01 displays used for this project. No additional GPS, tide sensor or AIS receiver is required; data arrives over Wi-Fi.

Open source & acknowledgements

Built for curious boaters.

TideLantern is a standalone project maintained by John Christensen (@Tikishark777). It reuses a compatible two-screen hardware pinout but is not part of SkyOrb; the original SkyOrb pin mapping and dual-display concept came from GulfCoastMaker.

Data and services: NOAA CO-OPS (U.S. high/low events), Open-Meteo (modeled marine and wind forecasts), and © OpenStreetMap contributors (place search via Nominatim). Libraries retain their original licenses.

Data can be unavailable, inaccurate or delayed. The tide curve is schematic, not an observed water level. Not for navigation, route planning, tide-dependent passage timing, or safety-critical decisions. Consult official predictions and forecasts before boating.
